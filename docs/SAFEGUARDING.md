# Child Safeguarding Note

I Matter is designed for adolescents and young people. This note explains
the safeguarding decisions built into the app and the responsibilities of
the adults who deploy it.

## Built-in safeguarding design

- **No contact features.** The app has no chat, comments, sharing, or any
  way for users to contact — or be contacted by — anyone.
- **No data collection.** Nothing a child writes leaves the device. There
  are no accounts, uploads, or analytics, which removes the most common
  digital risks (profiling, grooming vectors, data breaches).
- **Age-appropriate content.** All lessons, stories, and session plans use calm,
  positive, non-frightening language. Sensitive topics (bullying, unsafe
  secrets, reporting harm) are handled with clear "tell a trusted adult"
  guidance and never place blame on the child.
- **Help-seeking is normalized.** Every relevant lesson and story
  response points to trusted adults and the Help section.
- **Fictional stories only.** All names and stories are fictional or
  composite. Real participant names or personal stories must never be
  added without informed, written consent.

## Responsibilities before rollout (project staff)

1. **Verify support contacts.** `data/config.js` ships with the National
   Child Helpline (116), national emergency numbers (999/112/911), a school
   support prompt, and the programme office. Confirm these are current for
   your school and area before any child uses the app. **Do not publish unverified
   emergency numbers.**
2. **Brief facilitators.** Sessions on feelings, secrets, and safety can
   surface disclosures of abuse or harm. Every facilitator must know the
   school's and organization's child protection reporting procedure before
   running sessions.
3. **Respect digital privacy.** Adults must never require a child to show
   their My Space or journal. Progress counts stay on the child's own device
   and are anonymous by design — keep it that way.
4. **Shared devices.** Teach students to use *Delete all my data* after
   using borrowed devices.

## Responding to a disclosure (facilitator quick reference)

- Stay calm; listen without interrupting or interrogating.
- Reassure: "Thank you for telling me. This is not your fault."
- Never promise secrecy — explain you must involve people whose job is to
  keep children safe.
- Report promptly through your school's / organization's child protection
  procedure. Record facts, not opinions.
- Follow up: the child should never feel abandoned after telling.

## Content governance

- Changes to lessons, stories, or session plans should be reviewed by the program
  team against these principles: child-friendly, positive, non-shaming,
  non-frightening, help-seeking oriented.
- Keep the "safe/unsafe secrets", "reporting harm", and "child protection
  basics" lessons aligned with current national child protection guidance.
